Raft Paper Reading Notes

2026-07-22

Read Diego Ongaro's 2014 Raft paper alongside MIT 6.824. Much easier to understand than Paxos.

Why consensus

In distributed systems, multiple machines need consistent state. Master-slave replication works until the master fails. Consensus algorithms solve leader election without a central coordinator.

Raft's three parts

  • Leader Election — elect one leader, all client requests go through it
  • Log Replication — leader replicates log entries to all followers
  • Safety — committed entries are never overwritten

Leader election (5.1-5.2)

States: Follower (waiting for heartbeat), Candidate (campaigning), Leader (active). Randomized election timeout: 150-300ms per paper, I used 3-6s for easier debugging. Without randomness, elections deadlock when all nodes campaign simultaneously.

Log replication (5.3)

Heartbeats are empty AppendEntries RPCs. Flow: client sends command to leader, leader appends to local log, leader sends AppendEntries to followers, on majority ACK, leader commits and applies to state machine, leader notifies followers of new commitIndex.

Safety (5.4)

5.4.1 Election restriction: candidate's log must be at least as up-to-date as majority, checked by comparing last log entry term and index.

5.4.2 Commit rule: leader cannot commit entries from previous terms by counting replicas. This is the most subtle part. Without it, a committed entry could be overwritten by a new leader.

Implementation bugs

  • Timeout too short (100ms): three-way election split, no winner
  • Missed term check: old leader ignores higher-term RPCs, keeps acting as leader
  • commitIndex boundary: forgot 5.4.2, committed data overwritten by new leader
